          How these numbers are calculated

          Maintenance. Mifflin–St Jeor estimates resting burn from age, height, weight and sex. Cunningham and Katch–McArdle use fat-free mass, which suits lifters if you know your body fat %. All three are multiplied by an activity factor and can be off by 10 to 15%, which is why the measured option exists.

          Measured maintenance. Average calories eaten minus the energy stored as weight change, using your weight trend over the last four weeks. Stored energy is estimated at about 1,800 kcal per kg of lean tissue and 9,400 per kg of fat, mixed by how fast you are gaining. It needs 10+ logged days and two weeks of weigh-ins, and it is only as accurate as your calorie logging.

          Surplus and pace. A review of off-season nutrition for natural lifters (Iraki et al., 2019) suggests a 10 to 20% surplus and 0.25 to 0.5% of bodyweight per week for novices and intermediates, and more conservative for advanced lifters. In elite athletes, slower gain (about 0.16% per week) was around 85% lean mass versus around 65% at 0.38% per week (Garthe et al.). The intermediate and advanced bands here are an interpolation, not figures from one study.

          Cutting and maintenance. For a cut, the review by Helms et al. (2014) recommends a calorie level that produces about 0.5 to 1% bodyweight loss per week, with 2.3 to 3.1 g of protein per kg of lean mass and 15 to 30% of calories from fat. In athletes, slower loss (about 0.7% per week) kept more lean mass than faster loss (about 1.4%). Those protein figures come from lean competitors, so the app uses 1.8 to 2.7 g/kg of bodyweight when it does not know your body fat. Maintenance mode targets weight within about 0.1% per week.

          Protein, fat, carbs. Protein 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg per day (the 1.6 breakpoint comes from the Morton et al., 2018 meta-analysis), 0.40 to 0.55 g/kg per meal across 3 to 6 meals. Fat 0.5 to 1.5 g/kg. Carbs at least 3 to 5 g/kg to fuel training.

          These are population averages. Your weight trend is the final judge, and the Trend tab adjusts your calories from it.
